# Experimental sources — EUROFER97 loop and cavity data

Provenance for the experimental points overlaid on `density_vs_dose.png` and
`size_vs_dose.png` (generated by `RadCluster_2_1/output/*/make_figures.py`).

The figure script reads `InterstitialLoop.xlsx` and `Void.xlsx` and keeps rows with
`Material ⊃ "urofer97"`, `Type of Irradiation == "Neutron"`, and
`300 ≤ Irradiation Temp [C] ≤ 350`. That selects **29 points** in the density figure
(13 × ½⟨111⟩, 10 × ⟨100⟩, 6 × cavity) drawn from six papers.

Attribution comes from the `Paper` column of `InterstitialLoop.xlsx`, which uses
**merged cells**: one paper title heads a block of rows. Reading the sheet with pandas
leaves `NaN` on every row but the first of each block — the merge ranges (column J)
are the authority.

## Caveats — resolve before publishing the figure

1. **One ⟨100⟩ point has no traceable source.** `InterstitialLoop.xlsx` row 40
   (330 °C, 15 dpa, 4.67e21 m⁻³, 6.2 nm, BOR-60) sits outside every merged `Paper`
   block: the Chauhan 2021 merge starts at row 41. Its ½⟨111⟩ partner (row 41, same
   6.2 nm) is Chauhan 2021, so row 40 is almost certainly Chauhan too — but that is an
   inference, not a recorded attribution. Extend the merge or write the title into
   row 40 to make it verified.

2. **Chauhan 2021 contributes post-annealing data.** The paper reports EUROFER97
   neutron-irradiated at 330 °C / 15 dpa *and then annealed at 550 °C for 3 h*; the
   `Note` on row 41 says so explicitly. Rows 42–43 (7.0 nm loops) and one of the two
   cavity rows are plausibly the annealed condition. Plotting annealed microstructure
   as as-irradiated 330 °C data would bias the comparison toward larger, sparser
   defects. Split the as-irradiated and annealed conditions into separate rows with an
   explicit condition column, and exclude the annealed ones from the model comparison.

3. **Two cavity rows attributed to Klimenkov 2020 sit at 16.3 dpa**, which is the dose
   of the Klimenkov 2011 HFR campaign; Klimenkov 2020's own loop rows run 13.4–18.1 dpa.
   Confirm from the papers which one the 350 °C / 16.3 dpa cavity point belongs to.

4. **Probable duplicate measurement.** Rows 6 (Dethloff 2018, 300 °C, "15 dpa") and 16
   (Klimenkov 2011, 300 °C, 16.3 dpa) are both 4.00e21 m⁻³ at 14.0 nm. Dethloff 2018 is
   a *review* that re-tabulates earlier work, so this is very likely Klimenkov's
   measurement counted twice at two slightly different doses.

5. **Coppola 2019 dose.** `Void.xlsx` row 12 lists 250 °C / 16.4 dpa; the paper's
   abstract gives 16.3 dpa. (Outside the 300–350 °C figure window, so it does not
   affect these plots.)

6. **Dethloff 2018 "loops + black dots" rows** (8–9, 12–13 in the ⟨100⟩/½⟨111⟩ tables)
   count unresolved black dots alongside resolved loops and are therefore ~2× the
   loop-only densities from the same campaign. They are a different measurand from the
   model's loop density; consider marking them distinctly rather than mixing them into
   the same series.
