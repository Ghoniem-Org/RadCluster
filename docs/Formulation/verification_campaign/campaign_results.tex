\begin{abstract}
This report presents a verification and approximation study of the bin-moment
reduction used in the generalized cluster-dynamics model of
ferritic--martensitic steels, in response to the \S6.2 / Figure~8 critique. The
reduction is examined along each of its parameters --- the extent of the
discrete core, the number of logarithmic bins, the closure order and the
integration tolerance --- and then compared with the fully discrete solution of
the same equations on a domain small enough for that solution to be obtained.
Six observable quantities are reported for every configuration, against EUROFER97
neutron measurements at \SIrange{300}{350}{\celsius} where the comparison is
meaningful. A second group of calculations separates the two parameters the
first varied together, system size and bin count, and shows that the one
quantity failing to converge in bin count, the \halfp{} mean loop diameter,
converges in the extent of the discrete core. Three defects found in the course
of the work --- a physics channel absent from one solver path, a dose ladder
that scored at the wrong dose, and a corrupted moment reaching the interstitial
inventory --- are reported with the results they affect.
\end{abstract}

\section{Convergence of the Bin-Moment Approximation}\label{sec:conv}
%======================================================================

The bin-moment reduction replaces the discrete master equation over a domain of
$10^4$--$10^5$ cluster sizes with a hybrid representation: sizes up to
$i_{\rm discrete}$ are carried individually, and the remainder is partitioned
into $I_{\rm bin}$ logarithmically spaced bins carrying only the leading
moments, the intra-bin distribution being reconstructed from a shape function of
order $P$. Vacancies are treated identically, with $v_{\rm discrete}$ and
$V_{\rm bin}$.

No single calculation establishes that such a reduction may replace the full
system. This section proceeds through four groups, each varying one feature of a
common reference configuration. T1 refines the reduction's own resolution
parameter, $i_{\rm discrete}$: if the reduction is converged, the observables
must cease to move as the discrete core is enlarged. T2 varies the remaining
numerical parameters one at a time --- $I_{\rm bin}$, the closure order $P$ and
the relative tolerance. T3 tests a reduction in the physics rather than the
numerics: the quasi-steady-state treatment of helium against dynamic
integration. T4 is the verification proper, comparing the reduced solution with
the exact discrete solution on a domain small enough for the latter to be
obtained; T4b carries the same grids to the dose at which the measurements lie.

T1 to T3 establish internal consistency; only T4 establishes accuracy, and at a
single domain and dose. That restriction motivates Section~\ref{sec:sizes},
which follows the T4 ladder in dose and then separates the two axes T4 varied
together.

\subsection{Verification against the exact discrete solution}\label{sec:t4}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

The preceding groups compare reduced solutions with one another; this one
compares them with the exact solution of the same equations, on a reduced domain
$I = V = 4\,000$ chosen so the discrete system remains tractable. Deviations in
Table~\ref{tab:t4} are relative to run~D, not to experiment.

An important restriction applies. Production runs with
$\texttt{LOOP\_COAL} = 1$, enabling the \halfp{} loop--loop coarsening channel,
which was originally implemented only in the bin-moment path; the discrete path
accepted the flag, recorded it in provenance, and ignored it. The discrete arm
was therefore never the exact solution of the same equations, and the first
version of this comparison, appearing to show a closure error of 90 to
\SI{114}{\percent} in $d_{111}$, was measuring the absent channel. The channel
has since been implemented in the discrete path and the two verified to agree to
$7 \times 10^{-8}$
(\texttt{codes/Python\_Testing/check\_bm\_vs\_discrete.py}), but an exact arm in
the production configuration proved unaffordable: the term is $O(N_C^2)$ in the
populated sessile sizes and, even gated, consumed \SI{2.8}{\hour} on one output
step at \SI{0.014}{dpa} of a required 2. Both arms are therefore run with the
channel disabled, and the closure error measured here is not demonstrably that
of the production configuration. Without the channel the \halfp{} mean size also
pins at the mobility cutoff and its density exceeds the measurements 20- to
60-fold, so nothing in Table~\ref{tab:t4} may be compared with experiment ---
though this does not invalidate a verification, which asks only whether the
reduction reproduces the exact solution of the same equations.

Against the fully discrete solution the linear closure holds $d_{100}$ to within
\SI{1.1}{\percent} at every rung from 830 equations down to 86 --- a hundredfold
reduction in cost for a one-percent error --- and $N_{100}$ to
\SI{10.5}{\percent} at the coarsest rung, \SI{2}{\percent} at the finest.

The exceptions are informative. $N_{111}$ degrades sharply below C2, by
\SI{44}{\percent} at C3 and \SI{51}{\percent} at C4: the \halfp{} population is
peaked at small sizes, and once $i_{\rm discrete}$ falls to 25 and then 5 that
peak lies inside the binned region, where the closure represents it rather than
resolving it. $N_{\rm cav}$ is worse still at C4, \SI{+123}{\percent}. The
coarsest rung, which is the production resolution, buys its speed at real cost
in the two \emph{density} observables, while the \emph{sizes} remain accurate.

Convergence along this ladder is not monotonic: C1 and C2 are nearly identical,
C3 is worse than C2 in both $N_{111}$ and $d_{100}$, and C4 recovers in
$d_{100}$ while degrading further in $N_{\rm cav}$. A ladder that does not
improve monotonically is not evidence of a converged closure, and is presented
as an observation rather than as one. The likeliest explanation, tested in
Section~\ref{sec:sizes}, is that $i_{\rm discrete}$ and the bin partition
interact: the rungs hold the bin ratio $r$ fixed, but the discrete/binned
boundary moves relative to the peak of the distribution. The piecewise-constant
closure fails against the exact solution as decisively as against B4, by
\SI{+87}{\percent} in $d_{100}$ and \SI{-94.5}{\percent} in $N_{100}$.

\subsection{Conservation diagnostics and incomplete configurations}
\label{sec:incomplete}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

Two conservation defects run through the results above and are reported rather
than corrected. The Frenkel-pair diagnostic $\delta_{\rm FP}$ lies between 0.05
and 0.07 in all columns, and reaches 0.158 in the piecewise-constant columns,
against the model's own gate of $10^{-2}$. Closure error and conservation error
are distinct quantities and should not be conflated.

The second defect inflates $C_{\rm SIA,tot}$ by a factor of 17 to 25 in
fine-bin runs, $I_{\rm bin} \ge 16$: when the uppermost bin empties its zeroth
moment falls to the $C_{\rm floor}$ level while its first moment does not,
leaving $\mu_1/\mu_0 \sim 10^8$ in a bin spanning sizes 946 to 1001.
\texttt{\_floor\_bin\_moments} clamps $\mu_1/\mu_0$ back inside the bin on the
mean-size path, but \texttt{SIA\_content\_from\_mu1} sums the raw first moment,
so the corruption reaches $C_{\rm SIA,tot}$ unguarded. The six observables,
$\delta_{\rm FP}$ and the swelling are unaffected; what fails is the swelling
identity $S = S_I + \Delta J^d$, in which $C_{\rm SIA,tot}$ is $S_I$.

Five configurations did not reach their comparison dose and are absent from the
tables: the $i_d = 6400$ rung of T1 (\SI{0.079}{dpa} of 40 after
\SI{18.9}{\hour}); the $P=3$ lognormal closure of T2 (\SI{5.4e-4}{dpa}) and its
$I_{\rm bin} = 40$ column (\SI{5.70}{dpa}); the fusion arm of T3 with dynamic
helium (\SI{0.052}{dpa} after \SI{13.9}{\hour}); and the C0 rung of T4, which
grows stiffer the more of the frozen spectrum is resolved discretely. A sixth,
the fusion Case~1/2 arm of T3, was never runnable. The reporting tool refuses to
emit a metric for any of them, so the rule is mechanical rather than a matter of
discipline.

The null tests of preconditioner and thread count were not performed: they take
wall-clock time as the dependent variable and so require an otherwise idle
machine. Existing evidence stands in their place. In
\texttt{compare\_linsol\_results.json}, the one pair in which both arms ran to
completion (bin-moment, $N_{\rm eq} = 190$, \SI{0.01}{dpa}) agrees to nine
significant figures in $\langle n_i\rangle$ --- 24.563\,925\,879\,7 with
Woodbury against 24.563\,925\,898\,6 with Jacobi --- seven in
$\langle m_v\rangle$ and eight in the swelling, only the wall-clock time
differing, by a factor of 1.29.

\section{Effects of System Size}\label{sec:sizes}
%======================================================================

The preceding section establishes accuracy at a single domain and dose. This one
examines how that accuracy depends on domain size: first by following the
verification ladder of Section~\ref{sec:t4} in dose, then through a second group
of calculations, designated M, separating domain size from bin count.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Dose dependence along the verification ladder}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

Figures~\ref{fig:n111} to \ref{fig:dcav} show the dose dependence of each
observable across the ladder, ordered D, C4, C3, C2, C1, with the measurements
and the band spanning them overlaid. The \halfp{} mean diameter is excluded: with
the coarsening channel disabled it is pinned at the mobility cutoff and carries
no information about the reduction.

The trajectories terminate at two different doses, deliberately: C2, C3 and C4
were integrated to \SI{40}{dpa} to reach the measurements, while D and C1 stop
at \SI{2}{dpa}, the discrete arm having proved impossible to carry further at
any affordable cost. Below \SI{2}{dpa} the coarse grids may therefore be judged
against the exact solution, and above it they may not: where the extended
trajectories cross the experimental band there is no exact reference, so
agreement there speaks to the model rather than to the reduction.

\subsection{Separating system size from bin count}\label{sec:campM}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

The T4 rungs moved $i_{\rm discrete}$ and $I_{\rm bin}$ together, so a deviation
there cannot be attributed to either. The M calculations separate them: M1
varies the domain with no closure at all, M2R the bin count at fixed discrete
core, and M2D both independently, so every cell has a neighbour differing in one
axis. All run at production mobility ($i_{\rm mobile} = v_{\rm mobile} = 5$),
fission cascade, with \texttt{LOOP\_COAL} and \texttt{prec\_bw} at their
defaults in both arms, on one shared 45-point logarithmic grid to
\SI{20}{dpa}.

The reference domain is small: with $V = 1000$ a cavity is capped at about
\SI{2.8}{\nano\metre}, and the exact arm reports
$d_{\rm cav} = \SI{1.74}{\nano\metre}$ at \SI{20}{dpa}, \SI{62}{\percent} of
that ceiling. Both arms sit on the same truncated domain, so the truncation
cancels from the closure error measured, but nothing below may be compared with
experiment.

The increase is abrupt. Doubling the domain takes the exact arm from completing

three times. Domains of 4000 and above were not attempted.

\subsection{Origin of the residual mean-size error}\label{sec:d111}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

The M2R ladder varied the parameter that does not control this error: the
\halfp{} mean-size error converges in $i_{\rm discrete}$, not $I_{\rm bin}$, and
$i_{\rm discrete} = 50$ was held fixed across all nine rungs. At
$I_{\rm bin} = 16$ and \SI{0.05}{dpa}, against an exact
$d_{111} = \SI{2.9787}{\nano\metre}$, the error tracks the fraction of \halfp{}
content lying above the discrete core --- the fraction the closure must
represent:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rrr}
\toprule
$i_{\rm discrete}$ & $d_{111}$ (\si{\nano\metre}) & \% of content binned \\
\midrule
25  & 2.7232 \quad($-8.6\%$) & 96.6 \\
50  & 2.7744 \quad($-6.9\%$) & 91.2 \\
100 & 2.8128 \quad($-5.6\%$) & 83.4 \\
200 & 2.8692 \quad($-3.7\%$) & 70.6 \\
400 & 2.9403 \quad($-1.3\%$) & 47.7 \\
800 & 2.9813 \quad($+0.1\%$) & \phantom{0}8.0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

This is ordinary closure error, converging along the axis the M2R ladder held
fixed. Conversely, at $i_{\rm discrete} = 400$, raising $I_{\rm bin}$ from 4 to
8 to 33 gives 2.9615, 2.9503 and \SI{2.9245}{\nano\metre} --- slightly worse
each time.

Nothing is special about the \halfp{} population here: its mean size is about
147 interstitials, some three times the discrete core, so the peak of its
distribution lies in the binned region and is carried by the closure. $d_{100}$
and $d_{\rm cav}$ improve monotonically along the same sweep with about a tenth
the amplitude.

Four alternative explanations were rejected by measurement. A difference between
code paths is excluded by running the bin-moment path with $I_{\rm bin} = 0$ and
$i_{\rm discrete} = I$, carrying no closure: it reproduces the discrete arm at
\SI{20}{dpa} to within rounding, $d_{111}$ to $-0.000\%$. \texttt{LOOP\_COAL},
the leading candidate as the only channel implemented twice, is excluded because
with it disabled in both arms the deficit is unchanged, $-7.3\%$ and $-7.0\%$
against $-7.0\%$ and $-7.9\%$ with it enabled. A domain-edge artefact is
excluded because the exact spectrum decays smoothly to the top of the grid, size
1000 holding \SI{0.01}{\percent} of the content. Slow accumulation is excluded
because the deficit already stands at $-8.4\%$ at \SI{0.005}{dpa} and drifts
only to $-6.6\%$ by \SI{20}{dpa}.
